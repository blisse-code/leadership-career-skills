# Research Basis

What this skill's rules are built on, and what each source contributed. Read this when a user challenges a rule ("says who?") or when two rules seem to conflict and you need to know which evidence is stronger. Evidence strength: **Data** (study with a method), **Practitioner** (named hiring leader's stated practice), **Book** (established text), **Vendor** (tool company data; treat as directional, they sell a product).

## Resume screening and format

| Source | Type | What it contributes |
|---|---|---|
| TheLadders eye-tracking study (2018), 30 recruiters | Data | Initial scan averages ~7.4 seconds. Recruiters scan the left edge, pick out titles. Winners: clear section headers, bold titles over bullets (F/E-pattern), overview at top, white space. Losers: long sentences, multiple columns, keyword stuffing out of context |
| ResumeGo (2018), 482 hiring professionals, 7,712 resumes | Data | Two-page resumes preferred 2.9x for managerial roles, 2.6x mid-level, 1.4x entry. Two-page versions scored 8.6 vs 7.1 and got ~4 min vs ~2.4 min of review. Kills the "always one page" myth for leaders |
| NN/g, "Resumes for UX Career Changers" | Practitioner (UX-specific) | Action-verb bullets, [Verb][quantitative outcome][context] using [skill/tool]. No self-rated skill bars, no photos, no objective statements, no references. Single column, no tables, headers, footers, or columns for ATS. 10-12pt readable font, one accent color. DOCX scans best in ATS, PDF preserves styling, keep both. Order bullets by impact. Keep resume, LinkedIn, and portfolio consistent. One page is their rule for career changers, which does NOT apply to 10+ year leaders (see ResumeGo) |
| Jobscan ATS guides | Vendor | 99.7% of recruiters use keyword filters; 76.4% start by filtering on skills; resumes containing the exact job title got 10.6x more interview invitations. Single column, standard headings, .docx or text PDF, mirror JD language in context, no stuffing |
| Laszlo Bock (ex-SVP People, Google), XYZ formula | Practitioner | "Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]." The canonical achievement-bullet structure |
| Martin Yate, *Knock 'em Dead Resumes* | Book | Target Job Deconstruction: build the resume from what 6-8 comparable postings consistently ask for, in priority order. Resumes get a 5-45 second first pass; they are also the interview script and the final-round tiebreaker |
| Wendy Enelow and Louise Kursmark, *Modernize Your Resume*; Kursmark, *Executive's Pocket Guide to ROI Resumes* | Book | Headline + branded summary, core competencies block, achievement-first bullets, return-on-investment framing for executives |
| International CV conventions (TopCV and similar guides) | Practitioner | US: 1-2 pages (execs up to 3), no photo, no DOB. UK: "CV", 2 pages, personal statement, no photo. Germany: Lebenslauf, 2-3 pages, photo and DOB customary. Always check the target market |

## Leadership signals

| Source | Type | What it contributes |
|---|---|---|
| G.A. Rogers, "Leadership skills employers value most" | Practitioner | Six skills to show: track record, communication, problem-solving, collaboration, motivating others, confidence and authenticity. Always as concrete examples, never as adjectives |
| IxDF, "UX Management" | Practitioner | Strategic vs tactical UX management. Managing **up** (resources), **across** (product, engineering), **down** (hiring, coaching, performance). Impact measured through user metrics, business KPIs, qualitative insight, ROI |
| Peter Merholz and Kristin Skinner, *Org Design for Design Orgs* (O'Reilly, 2016) | Book | Design org models (centralized partnership), career ladders, team composition, design's place in the org. Vocabulary for org-building claims |
| Julie Zhuo, *The Making of a Manager* (2019) | Book | A manager's job is better outcomes from a group, through **purpose, people, process**. Useful lens for sorting leadership evidence |
| Arnold Lund, *User Experience Management* (2011) | Book | Making UX visible and valued inside the organization |
| Richard Banfield, *Design Leadership* (O'Reilly, 2015) | Book | Interview-based patterns on how design leaders build and grow teams, culture, and influence |
| Kim Scott, *Radical Candor* | Book | Feedback and critique culture; language for coaching evidence |
| McKinsey, "The Business Value of Design" (2018), 300 companies, 5 years | Data | Top-quartile design performers: 32 points higher revenue growth, 56 points higher TRS. Four drivers: analytical leadership (measure design like finance), cross-functional talent, continuous iteration, user experience across channels. Fewer than 5% of leaders could make objective design decisions. Use to frame business-acumen claims |
| NN/g, 6 levels of UX maturity | Practitioner | Absent, limited, emergent, structured, integrated, user-driven. Lets a leader state "moved the org from X to Y" in a recognized frame |
| Leslie Yang (design hiring manager), "How to show you're a senior designer" | Practitioner | Four seniority signals: **initiative** (didn't wait for the PRD), **product thinking** (root problem, success metrics, post-launch learning), **scope** (whole apps, verticals, multi-team), **innovation** (one envelope-pushing project). Red flags: no cross-functional evidence, solution without problem, no business impact, isolated feature work |
| Tom Scott, "Design Hiring in 2026" | Practitioner | Player-coach leaders dominate: companies want leaders who inspire "through the work". AI fluency (prototyping with AI, shipping front-end) is the sharpest current differentiator. Lead case studies with outcomes. Exec searches happen off-market through networks |

## Case studies and portfolio presentations

| Source | Type | What it contributes |
|---|---|---|
| Adobe Design, "How to ace a design leader job interview" | Practitioner | Show projects you **led**; your biggest contribution is design leadership, not design skill. Go deep on 1-2 projects. "Tell a messy story": obstacles, compromises, what didn't work. Credit the team: headcount, role types, skill sets. Prepare self-awareness answers (energy, leadership style, best conditions). Show business acumen: work at the confluence of user needs and business objectives |
| Helena Seo (DoorDash), "Three tips on design leaders' portfolio presentations" | Practitioner | (1) **Storytelling**: problem, process, insight, learning; plain language, no internal jargon. (2) **Balance**: team operations vs product innovation, vision vs execution, business vs user, breadth vs depth (T-shaped), leading from front vs back. (3) **The flavor of you**: what wouldn't exist without you, your brand, your craft in the deck itself, your motivation for this company. Never say "it's just" |
| Career Strategy Lab, "How to write a UX case study" | Practitioner | Seven sections: overview, problem, users, role, scope/constraints, process, outcomes/lessons. Write long first (2,000 words is fine), then compress to tweet-length headlines per section, then build. Descriptive title ("X User Research for Mobile Checkout", not "X"). Case studies feed resume bullets, cover letters, interview stories |
| NN/g, "5 steps to a UX design portfolio" (204 hiring professionals) | Data | 3-5 case studies. Show role, rejected concepts, evidence the solution worked, lessons. Hiring managers rarely read word-for-word, so scannability is required. They want to know "what isn't in the design and why" |
| Uxcel, "UX Manager Portfolio" | Practitioner | Managers show fewer, deeper examples (3-4). Add delegation, workflow improvements, team growth, hiring stories, frameworks you built |
| UX Collective (Di Donna), "Directors and heads of UX, do you need portfolios?" | Practitioner | At director level the portfolio is maybe 20% of the decision; process, management method, and culture fit dominate. Show how you shifted culture, grew people, built coalitions. Whiteboard sketches and conceptual models beat polished screens |
| Felippe De Pin, "The Design Manager Portfolio" | Practitioner | Show the spreadsheets: prioritization, OKRs. Show frameworks and rituals you invented, retention and team health, and credit the team |
| Verified Insider, "Do design leaders need portfolios?" | Practitioner | Leader portfolios are read by **non-designers**; design them for executives. Visual, data-forward, short |
| Leslie Yang, Part 1 | Practitioner | Panel format: ~45 min, 2 projects. 2-3 bullets per slide, 1-2 designs per slide. Say "I" for what you did, "we" for the team. Prepare "what would you do differently, what surprised you" |
| Barbara Minto, *The Pyramid Principle* | Book | Answer first. Situation, Complication, Question, Answer (SCQA). Executive skims lead with the conclusion |
| Nancy Duarte, *Resonate* and *DataStory* | Book | Tension between "what is" and "what could be" drives a talk. Turn data into a narrative with a clear turning point |
| Chip and Dan Heath, *Made to Stick* | Book | Simple, unexpected, concrete, credible, emotional, stories. One concrete detail beats three abstractions |
| Tom Greever, *Articulating Design Decisions* | Book | Frame design decisions in stakeholder terms; anticipate objections. Useful for the "how I got buy-in" section |
| Will Larson, *Staff Engineer* | Book (adjacent field) | Senior IC archetypes (tech lead, architect, solver, right hand). Maps well to Principal Designer scope: leadership without direct reports is still leadership |
| Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic, *Storytelling with Data* | Book | One point per chart, remove clutter, label directly. For outcome visuals |
| Mike Monteiro, *Design Is a Job* | Book | Designers are accountable for outcomes and must explain business value in client terms |

## LinkedIn practitioner posts (LinkedIn "How to showcase leadership in a professional portfolio" collection, as excerpted by the author)

| Idea | What it contributes |
|---|---|
| Three pillars | Share key decisions (choices, trade-offs, strategy); highlight collaboration (brought people together, resolved conflict, supported teammates); show measurable impact |
| Leadership signature (first-time leaders) | What you already lead without the title; what patterns others rely on you for; strategic value beyond pixels. Becomes the positioning line |
| Leadership proof file / decision map | Replace "look what I designed" with: the tension I resolved, who was involved and how I aligned them, the long-term impact (not just what shipped). "Leadership isn't about deliverables. It's about decisions" |
| Executive proof by level | Manager: built a team that shipped consistently high-quality work. Director: scaled design and aligned cross-functionally. VP/SVP: influenced executive decisions and tied design to outcomes. CDO: turned design into competitive advantage |
| Mandate, impact, leverage | For VP+ readers (CEO, CFO, CPO, board): **the mandate** (what you walked into), **the business impact** (revenue, retention, efficiency, position), **your leadership leverage** (who you hired, how you structured the team, how you raised the bar and earned executive trust). Operationalized craft beats described craft. Biggest mistake: "describing process instead of proving leverage". Clarity in 30 seconds, depth on demand |
| 5C + credits arc | Context, Contribution (role + three responsibilities owned), Choices (decisions and trade-offs, why), Collaboration (listened, aligned, unblocked, elevated someone else's idea), Outcome, Credits (name the team). Lead with context and contribution, then the hero image |
| Ownership without a title | Start with how you clarified or reframed the problem; say why you chose methods; highlight non-obvious decisions; show how you made sure it shipped; reflect on impact and what you'd change |
| Peer testimonials | A PM who saw you push for better decisions, a designer you grew, a researcher whose insight you amplified. Leadership is how others experience working with you |
| Career walking deck (9 slides) | Intro and direction; the person; operating system (strengths, mission, leadership philosophy, bio); Why you (results, not responsibilities); tentpole wins; two slides of third-party voices; tailored fit to this opportunity; 30-60-90 day plan. Send before the conversation |
| Portfolio of impact (Braven) | Micro-problems solved with before/after; human-centric wins (conflict, mentoring, empathy); AI used to remove a bottleneck; build in public |

## Confidence notes

- Recruiter-scan numbers (6-7 s) come from one 30-person eye-tracking study. Treat as "first pass is very short", not a law.
- Jobscan is a vendor. The direction (keywords and job titles matter) is consistent with every other source; exact multipliers are soft.
- The LinkedIn "top content" page on showcasing leadership in portfolios blocks automated reading; its themes are covered by the Uxcel, De Pin, and UX Collective sources above.
