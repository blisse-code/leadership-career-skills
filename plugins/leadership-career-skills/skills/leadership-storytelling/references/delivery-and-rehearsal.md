# Delivery and Rehearsal

## Pressure tests (run before delivering any story)
1. **Repeatability**: the listener's one-sentence retelling. Write it. If you can't, the story has no spine.
2. **Follow-up**: 3 hardest follow-ups with answer bullets ("How do you know it was your change?", "What would the PM say?", "What did it cost?").
3. **Specificity**: every abstract noun (alignment, impact, strategy) has a concrete example within two sentences, or goes.
4. **Time**: ~130-150 spoken words per minute. 60 s = ~140 words; 2 min = ~280.
5. **Values**: what the story says about the user without saying it. Matches intent?
6. **Attribution**: "I" for choices, "we/the team" for execution.
7. **Truth**: every fact traceable to the user; composites labelled.

## Spoken-script rules
- Sentences under ~20 words. Vary length.
- Put the number where the voice lands (end of sentence).
- Signposts for listeners: "Two things happened." "Here's the trade-off."
- Deliver the bullet version, not the script; memorise the opening line, the turning point, and the closing line only.

## Rehearsal plan (time-boxed)
- Day 1: say each story out loud 3 times; record once; cut 20%.
- Day 2: mock with a friend or with Claude as interviewer; get the 3 follow-ups.
- Before the interview: rehearse openings and closings only. Over-rehearsed stories sound recited.

## Panel and Q&A handling
- Pause before answering; restate the question in a few words if unclear.
- Answer first, then the story, then check: "Does that answer it, or should I go deeper on X?"
- If you don't know: say so, then say how you'd find out.
- If challenged: agree with what's true, then add evidence. Don't defend every inch.

## Remote panels
- Camera at eye level; slides with large type; share only the window.
- Say who you are answering ("Great question, Priya") to manage crosstalk.
- Keep a single sticky note: 3 story titles and your closing line.
