# Presentation Guide (leadership portfolio panel)

## Format facts
- Typical panel: 45-60 minutes, often 1-2 projects plus Q&A. Go deep on 1-2; too many projects makes the review feel rushed (Adobe).
- Mixed audience: design leaders, product, engineering, sometimes CEO/CFO. Design the deck for the non-designer; they decide.
- At director level the portfolio is maybe a fifth of the decision; process, management approach, and fit carry the rest. The deck must therefore also show *how you lead*, not only what shipped.

## Recommended 45-minute structure
| Min | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | Who I am as a leader | One-line leadership signature, scope snapshot, what I'm known for. Optional: my operating system (principles, how I run a team) |
| 3-18 | Case 1 (flagship) | Mandate, my role/team, 2-3 key decisions, how I led, messy middle, outcomes, lesson |
| 18-28 | Case 2 (contrasting) | Different leadership dimension (e.g. team build vs product strategy) |
| 28-30 | What I'd bring here | Connect patterns to this company's problems; optional 30-60-90 sketch |
| 30-45 | Q&A | |

Leave 15 minutes for questions; timing overrun reads as poor judgment.

## Slide rules
- One message per slide; the slide title is that message as a sentence.
- 2-3 short bullets, 1-2 visuals. Annotate visuals so viewers know what to look at.
- Show the thinking: decision maps, before/after org charts, rituals, frameworks, sketches, dashboards (sanitized).
- The deck's own craft is evidence of your standards; invest in it.
- No internal jargon. Unpack every acronym.

## The balance checklist (Helena Seo, DoorDash)
Across the whole presentation, show both sides of each pair:
- Team operations and product innovation
- Strategic vision and tactical execution
- Business impact and user needs
- Breadth (T-shape) and depth
- Leading from the front (clarity, direction, original thinking) and from behind (servant leadership)

And the flavor of you: what wouldn't exist without you, what you're known for, your craft, your personality, your motivation for this company.

## Talk tracks
- **60 seconds**: mandate, my role, the one decision, the result, the lesson.
- **5 minutes**: skim + 2 decisions + outcomes + what I'd do differently.
- **20-25 minutes**: full case.
Rehearse out loud and record once; never read from notes; never say "it's just".

## Likely panel questions (prepare answer outlines)
Leadership
- What would the team say about working for you? What would your toughest critic say?
- Tell us about a hire that didn't work out. What did you do?
- How do you run critique? How do you handle a designer who disagrees with you?
- What do you do yourself vs delegate? When do you step into the craft?

Business and influence
- How did you know it worked? What else could explain the result?
- Tell us about a time you lost an argument with product or engineering.
- How do you make the case for design investment to a CFO?

Judgment
- What did you say no to? What would you do differently?
- What's the riskiest bet in this story and how did you de-risk it?

Self-awareness (Adobe)
- What gives you energy and what drains you?
- What's your leadership style, and when does it fail?
- In what conditions do you do your best work, and how do you adapt outside them?

Questions to ask them: how decisions get made, where design sits in planning, what success looks like at 12 months, the hardest problem for this role, how the team describes its culture.
