# Case Types and Level Calibration

## Level calibration: what the case must make obvious

| Level | The case must prove | Hero of the story |
|---|---|---|
| Lead / first-time leader | I led without (or with a small) title: clarified an ambiguous problem, made the calls, pulled others in, shipped | The decisions I made when no one else had a plan |
| Principal / Staff IC | My judgment changed decisions beyond my team | The framework, standard, or direction others adopted |
| Manager | I built a team that ships consistently high-quality work | The team's reliability and growth |
| Director | I scaled design and aligned cross-functionally | The operating model and the alignment |
| Head of Design / Experience | The practice matured and the business noticed | The capability built and the design-business link |
| VP / SVP | I influenced executive decisions and tied design to outcomes | The business outcome and the exec decision shaped |
| CDO | Design became a competitive advantage | The strategic shift |

For VP+ readers, anchor every case on **mandate** (what you walked into), **business impact**, and **leadership leverage** (who you hired, how you structured, how you raised the bar, how you earned exec trust). If talking craft, show how you operationalised it. If talking hiring, show the calibre attracted and retained. If talking strategy, show where your voice shaped direction.

## Case types

Each type lists what carries the weight, the evidence that matters, and the trap.

### 1. Team / org build
- **Weight**: mandate, org design choices (centralised vs embedded, roles, ratios), hiring bar, rituals, career ladder, culture.
- **Evidence**: headcount path, hires and their level, promotions, retention/regretted attrition, engagement scores, delivery reliability, stakeholder satisfaction.
- **Trap**: listing rituals without showing what they changed.
- **Frame**: Merholz and Skinner's org models; Zhuo's purpose/people/process.

### 2. Design system / platform
- **Weight**: governance model, adoption strategy (carrot vs mandate), contribution model, trade-offs between consistency and team autonomy.
- **Evidence**: adoption (teams, products, % of UI), time-to-build, defects/inconsistencies, accessibility conformance, contribution counts.
- **Trap**: showing component galleries. Show the politics of adoption instead.

### 3. Transformation / turnaround
- **Weight**: baseline state (UX maturity level, NN/g six levels), the change plan, resistance, sequencing, quick wins that bought credibility.
- **Evidence**: maturity shift, process metrics (cycle time, rework), product metrics, exec sponsorship obtained.
- **Trap**: a revolution with no resistance. Name who resisted and how it resolved.

### 4. Product strategy led through a team
- **Weight**: how the problem was reframed, the bet, what was said no to, how the team was set up to execute, and post-launch learning.
- **Evidence**: business KPIs, user metrics, counter-metric, decision records.
- **Trap**: it becomes an IC case study. Keep the camera on direction-setting and team enablement, with 1-2 craft moments.

### 5. Influence without authority (Principal, first-time leader)
- **Weight**: the tension, who needed convincing, how the user built the case (data, prototypes, coalitions), what changed.
- **Evidence**: roadmap change, decision reversed, standard adopted, exec quote.
- **Trap**: "I convinced everyone". Show the objection and the concession.

### 6. Crisis / recovery
- **Weight**: stakes, triage decisions, how the team was protected, communication up.
- **Evidence**: time to recovery, incident metrics, customer impact contained, team stayed intact.
- **Trap**: hero narrative. Make the team and the system the hero.

### 7. DesignOps / process
- **Weight**: the bottleneck, the intervention, change management, measurement.
- **Evidence**: before/after cycle time, throughput, satisfaction of partners, adoption.
- **Trap**: process for its own sake. Tie to a business or quality outcome.

### 8. AI adoption (in the team's workflow or in the product)
- **Weight**: why AI fit the problem, guardrails, evaluation, human-in-the-loop, how the team learned, what was rejected.
- **Evidence**: time saved (with method), quality measures, error/hallucination handling, adoption across the team, product metrics.
- **Trap**: tool enthusiasm. Show judgment, including where the user chose not to use AI.

### 9. Agency / client leadership
- **Weight**: client mandate, multi-stakeholder alignment across client and agency, how the user shaped scope, how the team was run across accounts.
- **Evidence**: renewals, scope expansions, pitches won, client quotes, delivery outcomes.
- **Trap**: naming confidential clients. Use industry + scale descriptors unless public.

## Choosing which projects become leadership cases

Score each candidate 1-5 on: mandate size, user's leadership role (vs IC), evidence strength, business outcome, messiness (real conflict and trade-offs), relevance to target role, confidentiality safety. Pick 2-3 flagship cases (panels go deep on 1-2) that together cover different leadership dimensions, not three versions of the same win.
