# Evidence and Metrics

## Evidence hierarchy (strongest first)
1. Primary artifacts: dashboards, experiment readouts, decision records, PRDs, org charts
2. Third-party signals: awards, client renewals, analyst mentions, exec quotes in writing
3. Research outputs: synthesis, transcripts, study reports
4. Performance reviews, promotion packets, peer feedback
5. Recollection (label `[Inference]`, confidence Low)

Confidence: High (artifact exists, shareable), Medium (exists, restricted), Low (recollection).
Shareability: Public, Sanitized, NDA-restricted, Interview-only.

## Evidence table (required)
| Claim | Evidence type | Source | Confidence | Shareability | Redaction needed |
Every claim in the case needs a row. No row, no claim.

## Leadership metrics catalog

| Area | Metrics / observable signals |
|---|---|
| Business | Revenue, conversion, AOV, retention/churn, CLV, cost-to-serve, support contacts, time to market, contract renewals/expansions, pitch win rate |
| User | Task success, time on task, error rate, SUS/UMUX, NPS/CSAT, accessibility conformance, adoption |
| Team growth | Headcount path, hires by level, promotions, regretted attrition, engagement or eNPS, internal mobility, skills added |
| Operating model | Cycle time (brief to ship), rework rate, review rounds, design debt, research cadence, % of roadmap items with research, design review coverage |
| Alignment | Decision time, escalations, roadmap changes won, shared OKRs adopted, exec sponsorship |
| Platform / system | Teams and products adopting, % of UI on system, component reuse, contribution rate, defects from inconsistency |
| Maturity | NN/g UX maturity level before/after, McKinsey MDI-style practices adopted (analytical leadership, cross-functional talent, continuous iteration, cross-channel UX) |
| AI | Time saved (with method), quality/eval scores, error handling rates, human override rates, adoption |

## Integrity rules
- Baseline, result, timeframe, and attribution limits for every metric.
- At least one counter-metric or trade-off per flagship case. Single-metric victory stories are not believed.
- Leadership outcomes are often indirect. Say so: "The team shipped X; my contribution was the governance model that made the 3-market rollout possible."
- Never convert a guess into a number. Use a mechanism or `[Need Human Review]`.

## Confidentiality patterns
- Client: "a top-3 Southeast Asian telco" instead of the name, unless public.
- Numbers: ranges ("15-25%"), indexes ("baseline = 100, result = 131"), direction ("bottom to top quartile"), relative time ("in two quarters").
- Screens: blur or rebuild as wireframe; show structure, not brand.
- Keep precise: decision logic, trade-offs, operating model. These are what leaders are hired for and are rarely confidential.
- Add a line: "Details sanitized; happy to walk through specifics in conversation where permitted."

## Third-party voices
Ask the user for: a PM who saw them push for better decisions, a designer they grew, a researcher whose insight they amplified, an exec or client quote. Leadership is how others experience working with you; quotes carry what self-description cannot. Use real quotes only, with permission.
