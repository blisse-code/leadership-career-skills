## 1. Target Job Deconstruction (after Martin Yate)

1. Collect the target JD, plus 3-6 comparable postings if the user is running a campaign rather than a single application. Comparable postings reveal what the market consistently asks for, which one JD's quirks hide.
2. Extract, in the posting's own words: exact title, responsibilities, required, preferred, tools/methods, leadership scope (reports, budget, org size), business context, culture/values phrases.
3. Count repeats. Anything in 3+ postings, or repeated inside one JD, is Priority 1.
4. Build the Target Map table: Priority | Requirement | Keyword to mirror | User evidence | Gap.
5. For each gap choose: reframe adjacent evidence, address in cover letter, prepare for interview, or accept.
6. The top Priority 1 items decide the headline, summary, Selected Impact order, and competencies line.

## 2. ATS rules

- Put the **exact job title** string in the headline (Jobscan reports 10.6x more interviews when the title appears; vendor data, but direction consistent everywhere).
- Recruiters filter by skills and keywords (Jobscan: 99.7% use keyword filters, 76.4% start with skills). Mirror JD phrases in context inside bullets and the competencies line. Aim for high coverage of Priority 1 terms; don't chase a percentage by stuffing.
- Single column. No tables, text boxes, columns, graphics, icons, or skill bars. No content in page headers or footers (many parsers skip them).
- Standard headings: Summary, Experience / Professional Experience, Education, Skills or Core Competencies, Certifications.
- Standard bullets (• or -). Dates as "Mon YYYY - Mon YYYY" consistently.
- Include both acronym and long form once for key terms ("Design Operations (DesignOps)").
- Text must be selectable; hyperlinks must work.

## 3. Human scan rules (TheLadders eye-tracking)

- First pass averages ~7 seconds, scanning the left edge for titles and companies.
- Top performers: overview at top, clear section headers, bold job titles over short bullet lists, white space.
- Poor performers: long sentences, multiple columns, dense blocks, out-of-context keyword stuffing.
- So: bold titles, italic scope line, outcome or scale at the start of each bullet, 0.5-0.75 inch margins, 10-11pt body, one accent colour at most.

## 4. Length

| Situation | Length |
|---|---|
| Manager and above, or 10+ years | 2 pages (ResumeGo: preferred 2.9x for managerial roles; reviewed longer and scored higher) |
| Under ~8 years or user asks | 1 page |
| Executive in markets expecting detail (Germany, some public sector, academic) | up to 3 |
| Master resume (internal source) | whatever is needed |

Page 1 must carry the argument alone: headline, summary, Selected Impact, and most recent role.

## 5. File formats

- .docx parses most reliably in ATS portals.
- PDF (text-based, not scanned) preserves layout for email and direct hiring-manager sends.
- Keep both. Name files `Firstname-Lastname-Resume-TargetTitle.docx`.

## 6. Country conventions

| Market | Term | Length | Photo | DOB / personal | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| US, Canada | Resume | 1-2 (exec up to 3) | No | No | Anti-discrimination norms; no marital status |
| UK, Ireland | CV | 2 | No | No | Personal statement at top is normal |
| Germany, Austria, Switzerland (DE) | Lebenslauf / CV | 2-3 | Customary | DOB and nationality common | Formal; signed and dated sometimes; certificates (Zeugnisse) may be requested |
| Nordics (Sweden, Norway, Finland) | CV | 2 | Optional, common | Optional | Plain, modest tone; overclaiming reads badly |
| Netherlands | CV | 2 | Optional | Optional | Direct tone |
| Switzerland | CV | 2-3 | Common | Often | Work permit status often stated |
| Singapore, Malaysia | Resume / CV | 2-3 | Sometimes | Sometimes nationality, notice period | Stating nationality and work-pass status can help; expected salary sometimes requested separately |
| India | Resume / CV | 2-3 | Sometimes | Sometimes | Notice period often stated |
| Australia, New Zealand | Resume / CV | 2-3 | No | No | Referees sometimes listed at end |
| UAE / Gulf | CV | 2-3 | Common | Nationality, visa status common | |

These are conventions, not laws; verify with the specific employer or recruiter when stakes are high. For relocation, state it as a fact ("Relocating to Berlin, available Jan 2027", or "Open to relocation: Germany, Nordics") near contact details. State work authorisation only when it helps.
