## 1. Ganz: Story of Self, Us, Now
Structure inside each story: **challenge, choice, outcome**.
- **Self**: a moment that shaped why you lead the way you do. Specific, small, true.
- **Us**: the values and experiences you share with this audience (team, company, panel).
- **Now**: the urgent challenge to those values, the contrast between acting and not acting, and a concrete ask.
Use for team vision, all-hands, and "why this role" in interviews. "Here is who I am, what we have in common, and what we're going to do."

## 2. Simmons: six stories
| Story | Purpose | Leader use |
|---|---|---|
| Who I am | Earn the right to influence | Interview opener, first team meeting |
| Why I'm here | Show motives beyond self-interest | "Why this company", asking for investment |
| Vision | Make a future feel real | Strategy launch, low morale |
| Values in action | Make an abstract value concrete | Culture building, critique norms |
| Teaching | Transfer a lesson safely | Mentoring, onboarding |
| I know what you're thinking | Voice the objection before it hardens | Change programmes, skeptical panels |

## 3. Denning: patterns by purpose
- **Sparking action**: a short true example of success elsewhere, minimal detail, so listeners imagine it in their context.
- **Communicating who you are**: drama with revealing details, strengths or vulnerabilities.
- **Transmitting values**: believable characters; your behaviour must match the story.
- **Fostering collaboration**: a shared experience that invites others to swap stories.
- **Taming the grapevine**: gentle correction of rumours (only if false).
- **Sharing knowledge**: problem, fix, why it worked; invite better solutions.
- **Leading into the future**: evoke the desired future without over-specifying.

## 4. Duarte: what is / what could be
Alternate between current reality and the better future to build tension, then end with the "new bliss" and a call to action. Place a clear turning point where the audience must choose. In data stories, the data is the character; the insight is the turning point.

## 5. Minto: SCQA and answer first
- **Situation**: what the listener already agrees with.
- **Complication**: what changed or went wrong.
- **Question**: what this raises.
- **Answer**: your recommendation, then 3 supporting points, each with evidence.
For executives, lead with the Answer; SCQA is the scaffold behind it.

## 6. Kindra Hall: normal, explosion, new normal
Normal (how things were, with a specific detail), explosion (the moment it changed), new normal (what is different now). Good for LinkedIn posts, founder-style "why" stories, and customer impact stories.

## 7. STAR-L and CAR (interviews)
**STAR-L**: Situation, Task, Action, Result, Learning. Timing for a 2-minute answer: S+T 20 s, A 60-70 s, R 20 s, L 10 s. Actions must be choices with reasons, not activity lists.
**CAR**: Challenge, Action, Result. Tighter; good for 60-second answers and resume bridges.
Leadership upgrade: in Actions, show up/across/down (exec, peers, team), one disagreement, and one trade-off.

## 8. Heath: SUCCESs checklist
Simple (one core), Unexpected (break a pattern), Concrete (sensory detail), Credible (a number or a source), Emotional (a person the listener cares about), Story (a plot). Aim for at least four.

## 9. Picking the frame
| If the listener needs to... | Use |
|---|---|
| Trust you | Who I am (Simmons), Story of Self (Ganz) |
| Decide on a recommendation | Answer first + SCQA (Minto) |
| Feel the need for change | What is / could be (Duarte), Story of Now (Ganz) |
| Evaluate your judgment | STAR-L with decisions and trade-offs |
| Drop an objection | I know what you're thinking (Simmons) |
| Learn a lesson | Teaching story (Simmons), Sharing knowledge (Denning) |
| Remember one point | Normal / explosion / new normal (Hall) + SUCCESs |

## Mini example (fictional): 60-second CAR
> Challenge: Our banking client wanted a new onboarding app in 10 weeks; research showed the real drop-off was in document upload, a backend problem. Action: I took the evidence to the client's CIO with two options, a cosmetic redesign on time or a smaller scope that fixed upload first, and recommended the second. The product owner disagreed; we agreed to run the fix in one market first. Result: completion in that market rose from X to Y [user's numbers], and the client funded the rollout. What I took from it: the fastest way to earn scope is to be right in a small place first.
