# Career Walking Deck, 30-60-90 Plans, and POV Series

## 1. Career walking deck (9 slides)
Send before a conversation so the other person arrives with better questions. Tailor slide 8 every time.

| # | Slide | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Intro | Name, current role, photo (if culturally normal), one line on where you're headed |
| 2 | The person | A few human details: what you care about outside work (only what the user is comfortable sharing) |
| 3 | Operating system | Strengths, career mission, leadership philosophy (3-5 principles), short bio |
| 4 | Why me | Measurable results and differentiated experiences, not responsibilities |
| 5 | Tentpole wins | 3-4 signature projects or transformations, each one line + one number |
| 6-7 | In others' words | Real recommendations, review excerpts, client or peer quotes (with permission) |
| 8 | Fit | This opportunity's problems mapped to my evidence |
| 9 | 30-60-90 | How I'd start |

Rules: one message per slide, the slide title is the message, visuals over paragraphs, no invented quotes.

## 2. 30-60-90 day plan (for leadership interviews)
Purpose: show you already think like the person in the role, while signalling you'll listen before changing things.

| Phase | Theme | Typical content |
|---|---|---|
| 0-30 | Listen and learn | 1:1s with every team member and key partners; audit of product, research, design system, rituals; understand metrics and roadmap; identify one quick win |
| 31-60 | Align and prioritise | Share findings; agree design principles and success measures with product/eng; ship the quick win; fix the most painful process bottleneck; hiring plan |
| 61-90 | Build and commit | Operating model v1 (rituals, gates, decision rights); roadmap influence; team development plans; first measurable outcome reported |

Include: questions I'd need answered, assumptions labelled as assumptions, how I'd measure the first 90 days. Avoid prescribing big changes before listening; panels read that as arrogance.

## 3. Leadership POV series (LinkedIn or internal)
Builds the "ready for more" perception before the title. Each post is one small true story:
- Format: specific moment, what I noticed, what I did, what changed, the belief it taught me.
- Topics: what healthy design teams do, a trade-off I navigated, a framework I use to coach, what I'd change in design hiring, how I use AI with guardrails.
- 120-250 words, first line is a moment not a claim, no engagement bait, no invented numbers, no client names unless public.
- Cadence the user can sustain (weekly or fortnightly beats daily and burnout).
